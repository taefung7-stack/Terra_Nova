#G Although these ideas affect different areas of life, both show how social pressure influences our
#G behavior. Conformity bias makes people follow trends without questioning them, while social
#G comparison creates pressure to outdo others. Both can lead to unhealthy behaviors – wasting
#G money on products of questionable value or pushing children too hard in school.
Korean comedian Lee Suji has become popular on social media by making funny videos about
Korean trends. By making common behaviors look silly and extreme, she helps people think
about why they follow these trends.
(A)
Lee’s comedy shows two important ideas from psychology. The first idea is “conformity bias.”
This is when people just copy what others do without thinking for themselves. In one video,
she acts like an influencer selling a regular ceramic cup, calling it a “face slimming tool” for a
very high price. Her followers quickly buy it just because the influencer they like says it
works.
(B)
The second idea is “social comparison theory.” This happens when people always compare
themselves to others. Lee makes jokes about parents in Daechi-dong who are too focused on
their children’s studies. In her comedy, she makes fun of how parents go too far by sending
their children to hakwons for everything – even for basic life skills like using the bathroom.
Her funny exaggeration shows how competitive schooling has become in Korean society.
(C)
Lee Suji’s comedy helps viewers notice these mental habits in their own lives and think more
carefuliy about their own choices. Using humor instead of criticism, she encourages people to
question social pressures that might affect what they buy or how they live. By making us
laugh at ourselves, Lee Suji helps us become more careful shoppers and more thoughtful
members of society.
